    What the export actually contains

    Most free cover tools give you a flat image. A paperback cover is a single wrap that includes the back cover, the spine, and the front cover on one sheet, with extra bleed on every edge. CoverFit computes that sheet from three numbers: trim size, page count, and paper stock.

    If your page count changes, the spine changes, and the whole wrap changes with it. That is the step most DIY covers get wrong: the spine is not a design choice, it is a function of the interior.

    How to use it

    1. Set the interior first. Enter the same trim size and final page count you used for the manuscript. An odd page count matters less than being consistent with the file you upload to KDP.
    2. Design inside the safe area. Turn on guides. Keep text at least 0.25 in from the trim edge; anything in the bleed may be cut.
    3. Export both files. The PNG is for mockups and quick checks. The PDF is the one to send to KDP's previewer.
    4. Check barcode space. KDP places a barcode in the lower-right of the back cover, roughly 2 × 1.2 in. Keep that area quiet.

    Common questions

    What size should a KDP book cover be?

    Full width = 2 × trim width + spine + 0.25 in. Full height = trim height + 0.25 in. For a 6 × 9 in book with 320 cream pages, that is 13.050 × 9.250 in.

    How is spine width calculated?

    0.002252 in per page for white paper, 0.0025 in per page for cream. A 320-page cream book is 0.800 in. These are the figures KDP documents for black-and-white paperbacks; always confirm in the KDP previewer.

    Does CoverFit upload my files?

    No. Rendering and export happen in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and there is no account to create.

    Can I use the exported PDF for KDP?

    It is a single-page wrap at 300 DPI with the dimensions you entered. KDP still has to accept it in the previewer, and it cannot verify fonts, color, or account-specific requirements.
