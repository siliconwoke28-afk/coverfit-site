# Book trim sizes: which one fits your genre

> Trim size is the finished page size after cutting. It changes the feel of the book, the spine width, the cover file, and sometimes the printing cost. Pick it before you design anything.

Canonical HTML: https://coverfit.app/trim-sizes

## Fast picks

| Genre | Trim |
| --- | --- |
| Fiction | 5.5 × 8.5 or 6 × 9 in |
| Memoir | 5.5 × 8.5 in |
| Business | 6 × 9 in |
| Workbook | 8.5 × 11 in |
| Children's | 8 × 10 in |

## Sizes in inches and millimeters

| Trim | Inches | Millimeters | Usually used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 × 8 | 5.00 × 8.00 | 127 × 203 | Pocket fiction, novellas |
| 5.25 × 8 | 5.25 × 8.00 | 133 × 203 | Trade fiction, poetry |
| 5.5 × 8.5 | 5.50 × 8.50 | 140 × 216 | Fiction, memoir, self-help |
| 6 × 9 | 6.00 × 9.00 | 152 × 229 | Business, nonfiction, fiction |
| 7 × 10 | 7.00 × 10.00 | 178 × 254 | Manuals, workbooks, cookbooks |
| 8 × 10 | 8.00 × 10.00 | 203 × 254 | Children's picture books |
| 8.5 × 11 | 8.50 × 11.00 | 216 × 279 | Planners, letter-size workbooks |

## What the size signals

A 5 × 8 paperback feels like a mass-market novel; 5.5 × 8.5 feels like a literary trade paperback; 6 × 9 reads as business or serious nonfiction; 8.5 × 11 reads as a workbook. Matching genre convention reduces friction.

## How trim affects everything downstream

- Spine width depends on page count and paper, not trim, but the full wrap width includes twice the trim.
- Cover dimensions are computed from trim plus spine plus bleed. A larger trim means more pixels at 300 DPI.
- Printing cost is driven mainly by page count and ink.
- Interior margins need to grow with page count.

## Choosing between 5.5 × 8.5 and 6 × 9

Narrative books work at 5.5 × 8.5. Prescriptive books with charts or a professional audience work at 6 × 9. Choose the size first, then generate the cover with the matching trim.
