# Book cover design that survives the thumbnail

> A cover has two jobs: be legible at 200 pixels wide in a search result, and be printable at 300 DPI. Judge every draft at thumbnail size first.

Canonical HTML: https://coverfit.app/book-cover-design

## Thumbnail checklist

| Check | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Readable at 200 px |
| Author | Visible, not dominant |
| Contrast | 4.5:1 or better |
| Elements | Three or fewer |
| Genre | Signalled in one glance |

## Build a hierarchy, not a collage

1. Title: the largest element, usually 30 to 50 percent of the cover height.
2. Author: second largest, at the bottom or directly under the title.
3. Everything else: subtitle, series line, review quote, badge. Choose one and cut the rest.

Every additional element competes with the title. The most common self-published cover problem is not a bad image; it is five good elements fighting for the same space.

## Type choices that behave in print

Two families are enough: a display face for the title and a text face for everything else. Avoid very light weights, hairline serifs, and scripts below 24 points.

- Thrillers and crime: condensed sans, heavy weights, high contrast against a dark field.
- Literary fiction: serif or high-contrast display, generous spacing, restrained palette.
- Romance: script for one word at most, paired with a clean sans.
- Business and nonfiction: bold sans, two colors, one geometric shape.
- Children's books: rounded, warm, high-saturation, high contrast.

## Color is a genre signal

Readers sort categories by color before they read a word. Pick one dominant color and one accent; a cover with five colors reads as noise at small size. Test in grayscale: if the hierarchy collapses, the design depends on color alone.

## The spine and the back cover

On a shelf the spine is the only visible surface: keep title and author legible, match the front background, skip text below 0.25 in. The back cover should state what the book is, give one reason to read it, and leave the lower-right 2 × 1.2 in free for KDP's barcode.

## Common mistakes

- Designing at screen resolution. A 6 × 9 cover needs about 1,800 × 2,700 px before bleed.
- Forgetting the bleed. Background art must extend 0.125 in past the trim.
- Using a font you cannot license.
- Reusing a cover across page counts; the spine moves and the front cover shifts.
- Judging on a large monitor instead of a thumbnail.
