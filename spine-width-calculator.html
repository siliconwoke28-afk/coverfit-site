The formula

Spine width = page count × paper thickness. KDP publishes two thickness figures for black-ink paperbacks: 0.002252 in per page for white and 0.0025 in per page for cream. Cream is thicker, so the same book is wider on cream.

Example: 320 pages × 0.0025 = 0.800 in. The same book on white paper is 320 × 0.002252 = 0.721 in, a difference of 0.079 in. That is more than the usual cutting tolerance, so do not reuse a cover between paper stocks.

When the spine is too narrow for text

Below roughly 0.25 in there is no usable spine text. A 100-page cream book has a 0.250 in spine; after printing tolerance, a vertical title will look cramped or clip. Leave the spine blank and keep the color flat.

For spines between 0.25 and 0.5 in, reduce the type size and keep the label short: title only, no subtitle, no author. CoverFit hides spine text automatically below 0.18 in.
